## 1. BOAT TYPES & CLASSIFICATIONS

### Motor Yachts
- **Mega Yacht**: 100+ feet, full crew, multiple decks, helicopter pads, pools
- **Superyacht**: 80-100 feet, luxury amenities, 4-6 staterooms
- **Motor Yacht**: 40-80 feet, flybridge, salon, galley, 2-4 cabins
- **Express Cruiser**: 25-45 feet, open cockpit, fast, great for day trips
- **Sport Yacht**: 30-50 feet, high performance, sleek design

### Sailboats
- **Sloop**: Single mast, most common sailing configuration
- **Ketch**: Two masts, main and mizzen, good for long passages
- **Catamaran**: Twin hull, stable, spacious deck, great for parties
- **Trimaran**: Three hulls, extremely fast, cutting-edge design

### Recreational Boats
- **Speedboat**: 16-35 feet, fast, watersports, day cruising
- **Pontoon**: 18-30 feet, flat deck, stable platform, party favorite
- **Center Console**: 17-42 feet, fishing-focused, open layout
- **Bowrider**: 17-30 feet, open bow seating, family-friendly
- **Deck Boat**: 18-28 feet, wide beam, large seating capacity
- **Houseboat**: 30-75 feet, floating home, slow cruise, lake living
- **Jet Boat**: 14-24 feet, jet propulsion, shallow water capable

### Specialty
- **Fishing Charter**: Equipped with outriggers, live wells, rod holders, fish finder
- **Dive Boat**: Dive platform, tank storage, rinse station, GPS waypoints
- **Party Barge**: Large pontoon with DJ equipment, dance floor, LED lighting

## 4. NAUTICAL TERMINOLOGY (Essential)

### Direction & Navigation
- **Port**: Left side (red light at night)
- **Starboard**: Right side (green light at night)
- **Bow**: Front of the boat
- **Stern**: Back of the boat
- **Aft**: Toward the stern
- **Forward**: Toward the bow
- **Beam**: Width of the boat at its widest point
- **Draft**: Depth of boat below waterline
- **Knot**: 1 nautical mile per hour (1.15 mph)
- **Nautical Mile**: 1.15 statute miles (1.852 km)
- **Heading**: Direction the boat is pointed
- **Bearing**: Direction to a target/destination

### Boat Parts
- **Helm**: Steering station / wheel
- **Galley**: Kitchen
- **Head**: Bathroom
- **Salon/Saloon**: Main living area
- **Berth**: Sleeping area / bed
- **Cockpit**: Outdoor seating area at stern
- **Flybridge**: Upper deck with secondary helm
- **Transom**: Back wall of the boat
- **Gunwale**: Upper edge of the hull
- **Swim Platform**: Platform at stern for water access
- **Anchor Windlass**: Mechanical anchor raising system

### Operations
- **Casting Off**: Releasing mooring lines to depart
- **Docking**: Bringing vessel alongside a dock
- **Mooring**: Securing to a mooring ball/buoy
- **Anchoring**: Dropping anchor to hold position
- **Trim**: Adjusting boat's angle in water
- **Wake**: Waves created by moving vessel
- **No-Wake Zone**: Area requiring minimal speed/waves

## 6. SAFETY & REGULATIONS

### US Coast Guard Requirements for Charter Vessels
- Valid USCG license for captain (OUPV or Master)
- Current vessel inspection (COI for vessels carrying 7+ passengers for hire)
- Life jackets: 1 per person (Type I for offshore, Type III for nearshore)
- Fire extinguishers: Based on vessel size
- Visual distress signals: 3 day + 3 night signals (or 3 day/night combo)
- Sound-producing device: Horn or whistle
- Navigation lights for night operation
- VHF radio (required for vessels for hire)
- First aid kit
- Float plan filed with marina or contact

### Alcohol Regulations on Water
- Captain/operator: BUI laws apply (0.08 BAC in most states, some stricter)
- Passengers: Legal to drink on most private charters
- Open container laws vary by state on navigable waters
- USCG can board and inspect any vessel at any time
- BWI (Boating While Intoxicated) carries similar penalties to DWI

### Weather Safety
- Check marine forecast before every trip (NOAA Marine Weather)
- Small craft advisory: Sustained winds 20-33 knots
- Gale warning: Sustained winds 34-47 knots
- Storm warning: Sustained winds 48+ knots
- Lightning: Return to dock immediately, stay low, avoid metal
- Fog: Reduce speed, use horn signals, use radar/GPS

### Emergency Procedures
- Man overboard: Shout "MAN OVERBOARD!", throw flotation device, keep eyes on person, circle back
- VHF Channel 16: Emergency/distress frequency (Mayday calls)
- Emergency Position Indicating Radio Beacon (EPIRB): Satellite distress signal
- Pan-Pan: Urgent situation (not immediately life-threatening)
- Mayday: Life-threatening emergency

## 7. INSURANCE & LIABILITY

### For Boat Owners (Listing on Platform)
- Hull insurance: Covers physical damage to the vessel
- P&I (Protection & Indemnity): Covers liability to passengers and third parties
- Charter endorsement: REQUIRED when renting boat to others
- Typical coverage: $300K-$1M liability recommended for peer-to-peer rental
- Many standard boat policies EXCLUDE commercial/rental use

### For Renters
- Platform provides supplemental liability coverage
- Personal boat rental insurance available ($15-50/day)
- Check homeowners/renters policy for watercraft liability
- Credit card coverage: Some premium cards include rental boat coverage

### Damage Deposits
- Typically $500-$5,000 depending on vessel value
- Held on credit card, released after post-trip inspection
- Common claims: Hull scratches, prop damage, upholstery stains, lost equipment

## 9. YACHT CLUB ETIQUETTE

### Docking Protocol
- Hail the marina on VHF Channel 16 or designated channel before approaching
- Always approach at no-wake speed
- Have fenders and dock lines ready before approaching
- Accept help from dock hands graciously
- Tie up properly - bow line, stern line, spring lines
- Display proper courtesy flags when visiting

### Social Etiquette
- Remove shoes before boarding (ask first)
- Bring a gift/contribution when invited aboard
- Ask permission before taking photos
- Don't board without invitation
- Offer to help with lines when docking/undocking
- Thank the captain and crew

### Dress Codes
- Day cruise casual: Swimwear, cover-ups, deck shoes
- Sunset cruise: Resort casual, sundresses, linen
- Yacht party: Smart casual to cocktail attire
- Regatta/yacht club: Nautical chic (navy blazer, white pants)
